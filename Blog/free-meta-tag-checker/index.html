If you have ever pasted a link into a group chat and watched Facebook show a blank grey box instead of your page's image, or seen Google display a title you never wrote, the cause is almost always the same: something in your page's meta tags is missing, wrong, or being overridden. A meta tag checker is the fastest way to find out which one.

What are meta tags, exactly?

Meta tags are small pieces of code that sit inside the <head> section of an HTML page. They are invisible to a visitor scrolling the page, but browsers, search engines and social platforms all read them to decide how to display and index that page. A handful of tags do almost all of the work:

  • Title tag — the clickable blue link text in Google's results and the browser tab label.
  • Meta description — the short summary Google often shows under the title.
  • Canonical tag — tells search engines which URL is the "master" version when duplicate content exists.
  • Robots meta tag — controls indexing and link-following with directives like index, noindex, follow and nofollow.
  • Open Graph (OG) tags — control how the page appears when shared on Facebook and LinkedIn.
  • Twitter Card tags — the equivalent of OG tags, used specifically by X/Twitter.
  • Viewport & charset tags — technical tags that affect mobile rendering and text encoding.

Why checking your meta tags actually matters

None of these tags are visible on the page itself, which is exactly why problems with them go unnoticed for months. A missing canonical tag can cause duplicate-content issues. An old noindex tag left over from a staging site can quietly keep an entire page out of Google. A missing og:image tag can make every social share look broken. Running a website meta tag checker before and after a redesign, a CMS migration, or a new blog post is a five-second habit that prevents hours of debugging later.

Common meta tag mistakes worth checking for

  • Every page shares one title tag. Templated titles copied across a whole site waste ranking potential and confuse readers scanning search results.
  • Missing meta description. Google will auto-generate one from page content, which is rarely as compelling as one you write yourself.
  • A leftover noindex, nofollow tag. Extremely common after moving a page from a staging environment to production — and it is invisible unless you check.
  • No og:image. The single most common reason a shared link looks broken on Facebook, LinkedIn, or in a messaging app preview.
  • Duplicate or conflicting canonical tags. Two canonical tags pointing at different URLs can cause search engines to ignore both.
  • Title or description too long. Search engines truncate text past a certain pixel width, which usually cuts off around the character counts in the table below.

Does Google actually use meta tags?

Partly. The meta keywords tag has been ignored by Google since 2009 and is safe to skip entirely. Title tags and meta descriptions are treated as suggestions — Google frequently rewrites either one in the search snippet if it thinks a better match exists on the page. What Google does respect directly is the robots meta tag: a noindex directive will keep a page out of search results even if every other tag is perfect. This is exactly why running a meta tag checker online before a launch is worth the thirty seconds it takes.

Social meta tags: Facebook, LinkedIn and X/Twitter

Facebook and LinkedIn both read Open Graph tags to build a link preview; there is no separate "LinkedIn meta tag" — it reuses the same og:title, og:description and og:image tags. X/Twitter has its own twitter:card family of tags, but it will fall back to Open Graph tags if Twitter Card tags are missing. In practice, adding both sets of tags once covers every major platform a link might be shared on.

Frequently asked questions

What is a meta tag checker?

A meta tag checker is a tool that scans a webpage's HTML head section and reports on its title tag, meta description, Open Graph tags, Twitter Card tags, canonical tag, robots directives and other on-page SEO signals so you can spot missing or incorrect tags.

Is a meta tag checker online free to use?

Yes. Most meta tag checker tools, including ours, are free and only require a URL to generate an instant report.

Does Google use meta tags for ranking?

Google ignores the meta keywords tag and often rewrites titles and descriptions in search results, but it does respect the robots meta tag for indexing decisions.

What does the robots noindex or nofollow meta tag do?

Noindex asks a search engine not to list the page in results. Nofollow asks it not to pass ranking value through that page's links. A page left with noindex by mistake will not appear in Google at all.

Why do my links look broken when shared on Facebook or Twitter?

This usually means the page is missing Open Graph tags (used by Facebook and LinkedIn) or Twitter Card tags (used by X/Twitter). A meta tag checker will show exactly which social tags are missing.
