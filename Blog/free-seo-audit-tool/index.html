What an SEO audit tool actually checks

An SEO audit tool reads a page the way a search engine crawler does — not the way a visitor sees it. It looks at the raw HTML: the title tag, the meta description, the heading structure, image alt attributes, canonical tags, and the actual text content, then reports which of those pieces are missing, too short, too long, or inconsistent.

The output is usually a scored checklist rather than a single verdict. That matters, because a page can look great to a visitor and still be missing basics a crawler relies on — like a canonical tag, or an H1 that doesn't match anything else on the page.

Why regular audits matter for US and UK sites

Search behavior in the United States and the United Kingdom skews heavily toward mobile and voice queries, and Google's indexing is mobile-first in both markets. A page that passes a desktop-only glance can still fail a real audit on viewport settings, image alt text, or content depth — all things that affect how a page performs on a phone in Chicago or Manchester alike.

Running an audit before publishing, and again a few weeks after, catches drift: a meta description that got overwritten by a CMS default, an H1 that duplicated across pages, or a keyword that quietly disappeared from the copy during an edit.

Keyword density, explained properly

Search data shows this is the part people actually search for most — queries like google keyword density, keyword density google, and keyword density for google are common, which tells you there's real confusion about what the number means and how it's calculated.

The keyword density formula

The standard keyword density formula is simple:

(Keyword occurrences ÷ Total word count) × 100 = Keyword density %

Run this keyword density calculation for your primary term first, then repeat it for two or three close variants. A one-off word density check on a single term rarely tells the full story — you want to see how your total vocabulary is distributed, not just one phrase.

What counts as the best keyword density

There's no officially published best keyword density — Google has never confirmed a target percentage. In practice, most SEO checklists treat 0.5%–2.5% as a comfortable range, and flag anything past 3% as a maximum keyword density warning worth reviewing. Below is a quick reference:

Density rangeWhat it usually means
0%Keyword missing from the content entirely
0.1%–0.4%Low — may not reinforce the topic clearly
0.5%–2.5%Optimal — reads naturally, matches typical guidance
2.6%–3.5%High — worth rereading out loud for repetition
3.5%+Stuffing risk — rewrite for variety

How to run a free SEO audit

  1. Pull the page source. Right-click your live page and choose "View Page Source" — this is what a crawler actually reads.
  2. Run it through an audit tool. Our free SEO audit tool checks the title, meta description, headings, image alt text, links, and keyword density in one pass, entirely in your browser.
  3. Fix the fails first. Missing title tags, missing viewport meta, and "noindex" robots tags block indexing — resolve these before anything else.
  4. Address the warnings. Title or description length, missing H2s, and thin content are worth fixing but won't block indexing on their own.
  5. Re-check keyword density. Confirm your primary term sits in a natural range once edits are done, rather than assuming the first draft was correct.

Common mistakes an audit catches

  • Title tags duplicated across multiple pages
  • Meta descriptions left blank, so search engines auto-generate a snippet
  • Two or more H1 tags competing for the same page
  • Decorative images missing alt text, hurting accessibility and image search
  • Keyword repeated so often it reads unnaturally out loud
  • No canonical tag on pages with URL parameters or filters

Frequently asked questions

What is an SEO audit tool?

An SEO audit tool is software that scans a webpage's title tag, meta description, headings, image alt text, links, and content to flag issues that could stop the page from ranking well in search engines.

What is the keyword density formula?

The keyword density formula is: (number of times a keyword appears ÷ total word count) × 100. This gives you a percentage for a keyword density calculation on any page.

What is considered the best keyword density?

There is no single best keyword density that Google publishes officially. Most SEO audit tool checklists treat roughly 0.5%–2.5% as a healthy range, with anything above 3% flagged as a possible maximum keyword density warning.

Does Google keyword density still matter in 2026?

Google keyword density is no longer a direct ranking factor, but keyword density for Google still matters indirectly: it helps you spot thin content, missing topics, or unnatural repetition before publishing.
